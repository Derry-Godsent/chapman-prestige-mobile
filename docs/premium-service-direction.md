# Premium Service Direction: Chapman Prestige

## Design Position

The Chapman mobile experience will move away from a feature-dense dashboard and toward a quiet, image-led service concierge. The first screen should make one decisive action easy, establish trust through polished photography and clear service outcomes, and leave visual breathing room around every decision. The CPL logo sits naturally at the top-left on the app canvas, without a container, card, or decorative frame.

| Principle | Interface decision |
|---|---|
| One action at a time | A single full-bleed, image-led featured service is shown above the fold, with a single clear booking action. |
| Desire before mechanics | Service cards lead with the aspirational outcome—fresh wardrobe, restored sofa, protected home, pristine vehicle—before price or operational detail. |
| Calm, not sparse | The layout uses large image fields, deliberate 20–28pt vertical rhythm, compact metadata, and a limited number of high-signal modules per screen. |
| Native confidence | Bottom sheets, haptic primary actions, subtle 160–260ms fades and lifts, and persistent booking state replace ornamental effects. |
| Brand identity | The navy-and-silver CPL droplet is used as the app identity. The full supplied wordmark is used where its detail can be read, not compressed into tiny UI chrome. |

## Research Translation

The Chris B. experience demonstrates the commercial value of a focused discovery layer, clear product categories, real-time order updates, and fast routes to a saved basket or history.[1] Its public description also emphasizes order tracking, preferences, discounts, and saved delivery/payment settings as core service-app expectations.[2] Chapman should translate these mechanics into a premium home-care context: service exploration, confident booking, live job progress, saved addresses, loyalty value, and concierge support—without copying its visual language.

The first design pass will prioritize a vertical home flow: a quiet brand header, a large service moment, a small “care for today” card, an editorial carousel, and a visually rich service collection. Every module must justify its screen space; utility detail moves into the service and booking views.
